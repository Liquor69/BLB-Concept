The reality of treatment

Results are biological, not automatic.

Laser energy targets tattoo pigment, but a treatment outcome depends on far more than the device. Ink composition, pigment depth, skin response and previous treatments all influence how a tattoo changes over time. Strong consultations begin with this uncertainty made clear.
01Not every pigment reacts predictably to every wavelength.
02Fading is not necessarily full pigment clearance.
03Skin healing can affect later treatment options.
What to assess

Three factors behind a more honest consultation.

Use these topics as the start of an evidence-led discussion—never as a guarantee of a result.

01

Resistant pigments

White, flesh tones, yellows and some bright colours can respond differently because their chemistry is not uniform.

  • White & flesh tones: often contain titanium dioxide.
  • Yellow: may respond weakly to common laser wavelengths.
  • Light green & turquoise: can require highly specific wavelengths.
  • Red: may behave unpredictably.
02

Ghost residue

Fragmented pigment can persist as a faint shadow even when a tattoo appears substantially lighter in a before-and-after image.

03

Skin barriers

Repeated injury and healing processes can change tissue around retained pigment, increasing the need for a conservative plan.

Laser risk #04

Paradoxical darkening.

Some cosmetic pigments can darken after laser exposure rather than lighten. A responsible consultation identifies this possibility before treatment and documents the client's expectations.

  • 01Higher-risk colours: white, flesh, pink and some red pigments can shift toward dark gray or black.
  • 02Titanium dioxide: white ink may contain TiO2, which can change into visibly darker compounds after laser impact.
  • 03Plan conservatively: assess pigment history, consider a test spot, and review the outcome before expanding treatment.
The better pathway

Work with biology, not against it.

When laser treatment reaches a limit, the next step is informed assessment—not assumptions. A combination approach is built around the person in front of you.

Assess

Map pigment, skin status and treatment history before proposing a pathway.

Plan

Choose a measured protocol with clear checkpoints and documented informed consent.

Review

Evaluate the skin and the pigment response before deciding whether to continue or change course.
